Consent

Core Principle
Consent must be specific, informed, ongoing, and revocable.
Consent is not a one-time agreement or a checkbox to protect institutions—it is a continuous relationship between individuals and systems. For consent to be meaningful, it must be context-sensitive, actively maintained, and never assumed.

What This Value Requires of Systems
1. Clarity of Scope
o Consent must be requested with clear boundaries: what is being agreed to, why, and for how long.
2. Informed Participation
o Users must understand what they are agreeing to, in plain language, with accessible explanations of trade-offs.
3. Ongoing Affirmation
o Consent should not be presumed to persist indefinitely. Systems should check in, especially when contexts change.
4. Easy Withdrawal
o Users must be able to revoke consent at any time, without undue friction or punishment.
5. Granularity of Control
o Systems should offer layered consent, not all-or-nothing terms. Different actions should have separate, user-controlled permissions.

Examples of Alignment
?? Health App With Tiered Consent Settings
* Allows users to share only certain types of data.
* Prompts regular consent updates when sharing context changes.
?? Educational Platform That Explains Data Use
* Provides videos and text explanations before collecting data.
* Lets users (or guardians) set limits on retention and scope.

Examples of Misalignment
?? One-Time Consent to Broad Terms
* User agrees to a general policy covering dozens of unrelated uses.
* No follow-up or ability to revoke.
?? Platform That Buries Consent in Legalese
* Uses ambiguous or technical language to hide what is really being agreed to.
* Intentionally limits comprehension.
?? Dark Pattern Consent Withdrawal
* Makes revoking consent painful, hidden, or punitive.
* Creates a disincentive to exercising choice.
